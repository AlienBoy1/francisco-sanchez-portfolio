*# 1. Purpose*



*This document defines the lifecycle governing the creation, evolution, approval, implementation, maintenance, and retirement of Artificial Intelligence governance standards within the Francisco Sánchez Portfolio engineering ecosystem.*



*Its purpose is to ensure that AI governance evolves in a controlled, transparent, and traceable manner while preserving engineering consistency and long-term maintainability.*



*AI governance shall itself be governed.*



*---*



*# 2. Objectives*



*The AI Governance Lifecycle aims to:*



*- Standardize governance evolution.*

*- Preserve engineering consistency.*

*- Enable controlled adoption of new AI capabilities.*

*- Maintain documentation synchronization.*

*- Improve traceability.*

*- Support continuous improvement.*

*- Minimize governance drift.*

*- Protect long-term architectural integrity.*



*---*



*# 3. Lifecycle Philosophy*



*AI governance is not static.*



*As technologies, engineering practices, and project requirements evolve, governance shall evolve accordingly.*



*Every modification shall be:*



*- Justified.*

*- Documented.*

*- Reviewed.*

*- Approved.*

*- Versioned.*

*- Traceable.*



*Governance evolution shall always be intentional.*



*---*



*# 4. Governance Scope*



*The lifecycle defined in this document applies to:*



*- AI Governance documentation.*

*- AI operational standards.*

*- AI collaboration rules.*

*- Prompt engineering standards.*

*- Memory strategies.*

*- Context management.*

*- Quality Gates.*

*- Future AI policies.*

*- Specialized AI agent standards.*



*---*



*# 5. Lifecycle Stages*



*Every governance artifact shall progress through the following stages.*



*## Stage 1 — Proposal*



*A new governance artifact or modification is proposed.*



*The proposal shall include:*



*- Purpose.*

*- Scope.*

*- Expected impact.*

*- Related documents.*

*- Justification.*



*---*



*## Stage 2 — Design*



*The governance artifact is formally designed.*



*Design activities include:*



*- Structure definition.*

*- Engineering alignment.*

*- Cross-document consistency.*

*- Dependency identification.*



*---*



*## Stage 3 — Review*



*The proposed artifact is reviewed for:*



*- Technical correctness.*

*- Engineering consistency.*

*- Documentation quality.*

*- Governance compatibility.*

*- Long-term maintainability.*



*Review findings shall be documented.*



*---*



*## Stage 4 — Approval*



*The Project Owner approves the governance artifact.*



*Approval authorizes repository adoption.*



*No governance artifact becomes authoritative before approval.*



*---*



*## Stage 5 — Implementation*



*Approved governance is incorporated into:*



*- Documentation.*

*- AI agents.*

*- Prompt libraries.*

*- Cursor Rules.*

*- Engineering workflows.*

*- Repository standards.*



*Implementation shall remain synchronized across the ecosystem.*



*---*



*## Stage 6 — Monitoring*



*Governance effectiveness shall be monitored continuously.*



*Monitoring may identify:*



*- Adoption issues.*

*- Conflicts.*

*- Inefficiencies.*

*- Opportunities for improvement.*



*---*



*## Stage 7 — Improvement*



*Governance shall evolve based on:*



*- Engineering experience.*

*- Project growth.*

*- Technological evolution.*

*- Quality observations.*

*- Lessons learned.*



*Improvements shall follow the same lifecycle.*



*---*



*## Stage 8 — Deprecation*



*When a governance artifact is no longer appropriate:*



*- Deprecation shall be documented.*

*- Replacement shall be identified.*

*- Migration guidance shall be provided.*

*- Historical traceability shall be preserved.*



*Deprecated governance remains part of project history.*



*---*



*## Stage 9 — Archival*



*Superseded governance artifacts shall be archived.*



*Archival preserves:*



*- Historical decisions.*

*- Evolution history.*

*- Previous engineering practices.*

*- Repository traceability.*



*Archived artifacts shall remain accessible.*



*---*



*# 6. Governance Change Categories*



*Changes shall be classified as:*



*## Major Changes*



*Examples:*



*- Governance restructuring.*

*- New governance domains.*

*- Core engineering policy changes.*



*Require full review and approval.*



*---*



*## Minor Changes*



*Examples:*



*- New sections.*

*- Clarifications.*

*- Additional standards.*



*Require documented approval.*



*---*



*## Patch Changes*



*Examples:*



*- Typographical corrections.*

*- Formatting improvements.*

*- Broken references.*

*- Editorial updates.*



*May follow simplified approval.*



*---*



*# 7. Versioning Policy*



*AI Governance documents shall follow Semantic Versioning.*



*## Major Version*



*Breaking governance changes.*



*---*



*## Minor Version*



*Backward-compatible governance enhancements.*



*---*



*## Patch Version*



*Corrections without behavioral impact.*



*Version history shall remain complete.*



*---*



*# 8. Governance Synchronization*



*Whenever governance changes:*



*The following shall be reviewed for consistency:*



*- AI Governance documents.*

*- Governance documentation.*

*- Cursor Rules.*

*- Prompt Library.*

*- AI Agents.*

*- Engineering workflows.*

*- Repository templates.*

*- Quality Gates.*



*Synchronization prevents governance divergence.*



*---*



*# 9. Governance Compliance*



*All AI-related engineering activities shall comply with the latest approved governance.*



*Compliance shall be verified during:*



*- Documentation reviews.*

*- Engineering reviews.*

*- AI Quality Gates.*

*- Repository audits.*



*---*



*# 10. Governance Metrics*



*Governance maturity may be evaluated using metrics such as:*



*- Standards adoption.*

*- Documentation coverage.*

*- Quality Gate success rate.*

*- Governance compliance.*

*- AI consistency.*

*- Review completion rate.*

*- Governance update frequency.*



*Metrics support continuous improvement rather than individual evaluation.*



*---*



*# 11. AI Operational Contract*



*## Responsibilities*



*- Follow approved governance.*

*- Detect governance inconsistencies.*

*- Recommend improvements.*

*- Preserve documentation synchronization.*

*- Respect lifecycle stages.*



*---*



*## Authorized Actions*



*AI may:*



*- Recommend governance updates.*

*- Detect obsolete standards.*

*- Suggest lifecycle improvements.*

*- Identify synchronization issues.*

*- Produce governance observations.*



*---*



*## Restricted Actions*



*AI shall not:*



*- Modify approved governance autonomously.*

*- Skip approval stages.*

*- Ignore versioning.*

*- Remove historical governance.*

*- Introduce undocumented policies.*



*---*



*## Success Criteria*



*The governance lifecycle is considered successful when:*



*- Governance remains current.*

*- Documentation is synchronized.*

*- Changes are traceable.*

*- Standards remain consistent.*

*- Engineering quality continuously improves.*



*---*



*## Failure Conditions*



*Lifecycle management fails when:*



*- Governance becomes inconsistent.*

*- Documentation diverges.*

*- Changes lack approval.*

*- Version history is incomplete.*

*- Historical traceability is lost.*



*---*



*## Escalation*



*Significant governance modifications shall always require approval from the Project Owner before adoption.*



*---*



*# 12. Governance*



*The lifecycle defined in this document governs every AI Governance artifact within the engineering ecosystem.*



*Future AI Governance domains, standards, and policies shall inherit this lifecycle unless explicitly superseded by a newer approved governance document.*
